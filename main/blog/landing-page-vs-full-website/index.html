Landing Page vs Full Website: Which One Does Your Business Actually Need?

6 min read · 19 Feb 2026

When you start looking for a web designer, you will quickly run into two different things being sold to you: a landing page (sometimes called a one-page site) and a full multi-page website. The price difference can be significant — RM 1,300 versus RM 5,000 or more. So which one does your business actually need?

The answer depends on what you want your website to do. Most Malaysian SMEs — especially in trades, services, and F&B — need a landing page. Not because it is the cheaper option, but because it is genuinely the right tool for the job.

What Is a Landing Page?

A landing page is a single-page website where all the information about your business lives on one scrollable page. Visitors arrive, scroll through your services, see your photos and testimonials, and then take action — usually messaging you on WhatsApp or calling.

Everything on a landing page is designed around one goal: get the visitor to contact you.

A typical landing page for a Malaysian SME includes:

  • A headline that states what you do and where you operate
  • A brief services section
  • Photos of your work or premises
  • Testimonials from past clients
  • Contact details and a prominent WhatsApp button
  • A Google Maps embed showing your location

That is enough for most businesses to convert a visitor into an enquiry.

What Is a Full Website?

A full website has multiple pages — usually a Home page, Services page, About Us, Contact, and sometimes a Gallery, FAQ, or Blog. Each page has its own URL and can rank separately in Google search.

Full websites make sense when:

  • You have a large number of distinct services that need separate explanation
  • You want to rank for multiple different search terms across different pages
  • You publish regular content (like blog articles) that supports your SEO
  • You have a complex customer journey that requires multiple decision points

Most Malaysian SMEs Do Not Need a Full Website

We say this directly because we have seen business owners spend RM 5,000 to RM 8,000 on a full agency website with seven pages — and then not update it once after launch. Six months later, the "News" section still shows a post from the week they launched, the staff page has people who left the company, and the site is ranking for almost nothing because there is no ongoing content.

A well-built landing page that accurately describes what you do, shows real photos of your work, and has a visible WhatsApp button will out-convert that neglected full website every time.

In our experience, the businesses that benefit most from a full website are those that are already consistently generating business through a landing page and want to grow further — or those with a specific content or SEO strategy in place.

When You Should Choose a Landing Page

Choose a landing page if you can answer yes to most of these:

  • Your business does one main thing (e.g. air conditioning service, catering, hardware supply)
  • Your clients are in a specific geographic area (e.g. Klang Valley, Johor Bahru)
  • Clients typically contact you via WhatsApp or phone, not through an online form
  • You do not have the time or inclination to write and maintain blog articles
  • You want to be up and running within a week

When You Should Choose a Full Website

Choose a full website if:

  • You have 5 or more meaningfully distinct service lines that each need their own explanation
  • You are building a content or SEO strategy and plan to publish articles regularly (like this one)
  • You sell products or need booking functionality
  • You are building a brand that needs a dedicated About page, team profiles, and detailed credentials
  • You have the budget and a plan to maintain it

A Note on Growing Into a Full Website

Starting with a landing page does not mean you are locked out of a full website later. Many of our clients start with a landing page to establish their online presence quickly, generate their first leads, and prove that web traffic converts to enquiries. Once that is confirmed, expanding to a full site is a natural next step — and a much easier decision to make with evidence in hand.
